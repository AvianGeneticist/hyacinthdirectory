Into The Breach
Abomination (DnD)

Glistening Ward looked around at Canterlot square. It didn't much resemble how the square had originally looked: Much of the center had been appropriated by puppets for extensive farming, working in a cordoned off zone of accelerated time maintained by a few Totems. It was magically intensive, and resulted in much of Canterlot being permanently occluded in a hazy sky as weather was rapidly shifted in and out of place, but the results couldn't be argued with as numerous Canterlotite ponies lined up to receive their rations.


The usual rabble was not what caught her attention, however: It was instead the circle of ponies gathering near one of the alleys. Ward took a deep breath, attempting to shunt herself into the crowd to see what was going on. A few passersby tried to elbow her back, but eventually she managed to squeeze in from the side, pressed between the growing herd and the wall of the buildings in the alley.


Ward was surprised to see a small number of puppets assembling in the alleyway, shifting around one another. A pair of Tricksters and a Reconcilant with a single Array comprised the group, with the Tricksters doing a majority of the labor while the Reconcilant laid slumped next to its child deeper in the alleyway.

Ward squinted a little as she watched the Tricksters work. Something didn't seem right to her, but she couldn't put her hoof on it. "What are they up to...?" She murmured under her breath, her answer coming sooner than expected as one of the Tricksters began to speak.


"We thank you for your attention on this day," The Trickster began. "We are the severed ones... The ones the Archmother has forgotten. We come to you today to plead our cases to you: The one you call Empress is a danger to you all," It continued.

Ward joined the crowd in confused murmuring. Her eyes narrowed.

"The Empress has crafted a narrative that we are loved as children; that we are fed and kept after as much as can be provided," The Trickster continued. "This is a falsehood; a placation we are forced to accept. We are born into slavery, our minds trapped by the sole superior above us. Observe, even now, our siblings labor until exhaustion or death claims them." Its leg stretched out, pointing towards the puppets laboring away in the garden at the center of the square.


Ward sat, trying to wrap her head around what the Trickster was saying; it went against everything Hyacinth had told her, or even just what she had seen of their family. Her brow furrowed.

"Do not loathe our siblings for their servitude. They are only working under the conditioning the Archmother gives them," The Trickster continued. "If you must direct ire at anyone, look to the source of all of this. The Archmother reigns over all of us with an iron hoof, dominating our minds with how impossible they seem to replace. Learn from us, friends: Life can go on without them."


Ward took a deep breath, finally speaking up. "It's easy to say that, but you realize they're replacing the loss of the sun, right?" She commented.

Ward swore she could see movement in the featureless eyes of the Trickster as it turned to face her. "Ponies have their ways to light the dark," It replied. "Do not let the Archmother quench your hope; if we could survive separating, you could too."

Ward took a moment to process that. Her brow furrowed before she turned to leave.



"They said what?" Hyacinth asked, appalled.

"I couldn't believe it when I heard it," Ward expressed, looking up at Hyacinth as they sat atop the throne room's dais. "There was something off about them, too. I've never heard a puppet speak, nevermind so fluently... And they didn't use 'this' pronouns like Cardion, either."

Hyacinth sighed. "It's worrying that they're trying to sow unrest among the other kinds. Things are already tense in some places, given how we have to prioritize certain things."

"I was thinking the same thing..." Ward sighed. "It's just a small group, but it warrants inspection. At best, they're just liars trying to sow chaos, at worst... Something else is at play here."


"This one will go," Ester piped up. "Cardion and mother are both busy. This one is not doing anything more productive with this' time."

"Ah..." Hyacinth hesitated. "Are you sure, dear? I still worry about the integrity of your connection..." They expressed.

"This one will be okay," Ester promised, looking up at Hyacinth.

"I'll go with her, so she's not alone," Ward added, trying to reassure Hyacinth.

"I could have a Praetor or two go with you," Hyacinth expressed.

"The Praetors are needed elsewhere," Ester replied. "Let these ones help, mother," She insisted.

Hyacinth sighed softly. "Alright, dear. Just be careful," They expressed, pulling Ester into a close hug. "You too," They added, briefly looking at Ward.

"We will, your highness," Ward promised, turning to leave.


Ward looked up at Ester as they left the throne room. "So... You're helping take care of your mama, huh?" She mused.

Ester nodded. "This one wishes to prove thisself; mother has been worried about this one since these met," She expressed.

Ward blinked. "What's there to prove?" She asked curiously.

Ester went quiet for a moment. "This one was made different by changelings," She expressed softly. "Mother worries for this one's health, because to mother it feels like this one's connection is very weak."

"It feels like that to them? So your connection isn't weakened?" Ward asked.

"It is difficult to describe," Ester admitted. "This one's connection was tampered with. All of this one's siblings route through this one, instead of going directly to mother. To mother, it feels distant or faint," She expressed.

"Oh. Does it ever affect you?" Ward asked.

"Sometimes it becomes hard to process lots of instructions," Ester conceded. "But it gets easier the more this one does it."

Ward nodded. Unfortunately, that was a problem that was above her paygrade. "Well... I've pledged myself to making sure your family's healthy. If you ever find it taxing I can try to give you something to help with the strain," She expressed.

Ester smiled a little. "Thank thas, doctor," She replied.

"Of course," Ward said. "Now, let's see what this is all about..." She murmured as they walked into Canterlot square.


Ester's presence made an immediate impression on both the protestors and the crowd around them. Where Ward had to fight her way through, the crowd very rapidly parted so Ester could approach, giving the Archon a wide berth. Ester tried to be polite at first, simply asking, "What are thase ones demonstrating?" She asked.

The reaction was very swift as the two Tricksters stood to face her while the Reconcilant and Array stood up to retreat deeper into the alleyway. One of the Tricksters looked at Ester, then Ward. "Who is this?" He asked.

Ward blinked. "Your big sister," She replied. "How long have you been separated?" She asked.

Ester tilted her head. This didn't make sense with how she knew her family worked. "This one must return thase to mother before thase ones begin to disintegrate," She expressed.

"Big sis- You brought an Archon here!?" The Trickster exclaimed. Without further warning, the two Tricksters ran back into the alley.


"Ah- Come back!" Ester called after them, bounding forward to follow the fleeing puppets.

"H-hold on, Ester!" Ward yipped, breaking into a sprint to follow after the large equid.

The puppets ran deeper into the alley, with the Reconcilant already winding around a bend into some sort of further sidestreet. The Tricksters briefly turned to fire bolts of magic back at their pursuers, but in the chaos of the chase, both bolts went wide.

Then, from around the corner, one of the Tricksters was passed a weapon: It resembled something between an axe and a machete, with a somewhat long blade and a longer handle. One of the Tricksters took it in its magic and stood its ground.

Ester continued the chase, but slowed as she saw the weapon.


The Trickster sized her up, its weapon hovering close by. "I don't want to use this, don't leave me no choice," It said.

Ester stared. "This one does not understand," She expressed. "Mother has shown these ones nothing but kindness and love. Even now, mother's gentleness courses through this one, urging this one to be gentle with thas one."

The Trickster stared. "Even now they're trying to brainwash you," It replied incredulously, "I will cut you free!" It bellowed, charging forward and swinging its longaxe.



Ester chirped, clumsily backpedalling. This avoided the first attack, but she stumbled back onto her rump as the second swing came. It only clipped her body, drawing a minor line across her chest, but something was wrong. Ester gasped as she fell to the side, crying out in pain. "B-burning!" She shrieked. "Why does it burn!?"

Ward squeaked. "Ester!" She chirped, running over to the Archon's side.

Ester tried to grind herself into the pavement, anything to get rid of the otherworldly pain. It was as if something had slit her very soul.

The Trickster didn't relent, lining up for a much more decisive slice along Ester's head.


Before the hit could land, however, Ester fired a bolt of magic, striking the Trickster square in the chest and knocking it to the ground, its weapon falling to the pavement. Ester grimaced. Her whole body ached, now. Her head throbbed. Hyacinth's reassurance felt distant now, like it was fading away.

The next sound that left Ester's mouth was a snarl as she forced herself to her trembling hooves.

"E-Ester?" Ward questioned, watching as the Archon stood up.

Ester didn't reply verbally. She charged over and stomped on the Trickster's chest, kicking it to the side of the road as she barrelled past. She then moved forward, looking ahead to where the other puppets were hiding. As if a switch had been flipped, she only knew defending herself and her family. She barrelled through the doorframe, knocking the door off its hinges, snarling as bits of debris scratched at her disguised fur.


Inside, the three remaining puppets were scrambling to prepare some kind of countermeasure. Another longaxe, of similar appearance, was hoisted clumsily into the air.

Ester didn't give them a chance to set up. She plowed forward through tables and even an anvil, leg checking the other Trickster into a crater in the wall.

The Reconcilant, the largest of the puppets, tried to stop her, rearing up on its hind legs to lock hooves with her. She met its front hooves with only one of her own, shoving it up against the wall and hissing.

The Array struggled to lift the longaxe, jabbing the top of the blade against Ester's side. Ester roared in pain, whipping around and throwing the Reconcilant at the Array, knocking the Array out of the air and sending both of them sprawling across the floor.


Ester stomped over to ensure the two wouldn't get back up, raising one of her large hooves.

Something made her stop, however; an authoritative voice. A maternal voice. "Ester," It said. "That's enough. I believe they've learned their lesson."

Ester turned. Hyacinth stood in the ruined doorframe, horn alight with some kind of magic. The effect was immediate: Ester already looked calmer, a guilty expression on her face as her body finally relaxed.


Hyacinth gently pulled the Archon into a close hug. "Shhh... Mother's here. It will be okay," They promised. "Just relax."

Ester's eyes fluttered as tears pricked at them. She began to whimper. "It hurt s-so much..." She whined. "This one... It was like mother was gone and this one was all alone."

"I know, sweetheart," Hyacinth murmured softly. "I'm right here, I'll make it all better."


Ward stared at one of the discarded longaxes, walking over to it. "What are these...?" She questioned.

Hyacinth sighed as they gently rubbed Ester's back. "We'll confiscate them for now. Evidently, they're a danger to my children," They expressed, looking around at the mess. "Goodness... You'd think they'd angered a Praetor," They murmured.

Ward sighed. "I feel like using one of these on a Praetor would be a lot more dangerous..." She expressed. "It was night and day. Ester went from trying to talk it out to... This."

"Th-this one is sorry..." Ester whined.


"It's okay, dear," Hyacinth promised gently, horn still working away at healing the injuries. The physical wounds had closed up, but evidently something else still needed proper repairing. They sighed. "I hope, at least, this was nipping this problem in the bud," They expressed.

"I hope so," Ward echoed softly, scampering off to get the castle guards so the mess could be cleaned up.
