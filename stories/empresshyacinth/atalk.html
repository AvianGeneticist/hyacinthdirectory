Hyacinth looked down at the cuffed puppet across from them, watching as it came to. One of the Tricksters; the one they'd found in the street.

The Trickster shook its head, cumbersomely rolling onto its front. It jumped as Hyacinth greeted them.

"Good morning, aberrant one," They greeted simply. "I hope you rested well. We did our best to fix you up from Ester's outburst."

The Trickster looked up at them fearfully. "You... You fixed me? What fo- wait, where are the others? What did you do with-" It began.

Hyacinth interrupted it. "The other two are being tended to in their own cells. Little Einkorn is being tended to in the castle day care right now," They answered. "Do not think using a child as part of your 'movement' will go unpunished."


The Trickster shivered. "What is to be my fate?" It asked.

"I wish to listen," Hyacinth replied simply. "I have questions which I want you to answer. Your sentence will not be lessened, but I will try to reward cooperation with what comforts I can spare."

The Trickster stared at them for a moment. Hyacinth didn't bother waiting for an answer.

"Before I begin, however... I must commend you on your costumes. They are quite convincing, particularly if you don't know what inconsistencies to look for. Dying your entire body must have taken some significant effort."

The "Trickster" flinched. "I-it... How did you know?" It asked.

"I would regret telling you anything more detailed, but the simplest fact is already common knowledge in some places: My children die without some kind of connection back to me. We would not be talking here, were you truly a separated child of mine," Hyacinth commented. "I have seen too many of them return to the earth to forget that," They expressed, closing their eyes for a moment.


The unicorn looked at them for a moment, watching them carefully, but not speaking out of turn.

Hyacinth opened their eyes once more. "Tell me," They started. "Simple discontent could not have motivated the creation of an entirely new class of weapon for the sole purpose of hurting my family: What did we do to you?" They asked, lumbering closer to the bars and kneeling to be a bit more on the unicorn's level.

The unicorn stared back at Hyacinth for a moment, not daring to speak until they were prepared. "I... We, were a farming family. You requisitioned our land," The unicorn replied. "Our whole livelihood was ripped away from us in minutes."

Hyacinth's expression softened. "Ah..." They murmured, looking away for a moment. "I was a farmer too, you know. Before all of this," They looked back at the unicorn. "Not this rule, I mean. Before I became... A monster."

The unicorn stared. "Haven't you always been like that?" They asked.


Hyacinth cracked a smile. "For as long as you've been alive at least, no doubt," They commented with a weak lilt of humour in their tone. "But my story stretches beyond that. I lived on a farm for about a decade and a half before..." They wafted their hand over their chest. "This began."

The unicorn tilted their head. "So you were a child then?" They asked.

"A young mare towards the end, but yes," Hyacinth nodded. "My little sister and I worked that farm quite a lot in our younger years. Our family had ties in the castle, so we were an... 'Order farm' of sorts. Consistently, we would get requests from the castle for certain crops," They explained, before sighing. "I know that's not the same as having your land taken away from you, but I hope you at least see that I understand the weight behind such a decision. One day I hope to return that land to you, assuming you and your family are free to work it," They expressed.


The unicorn stared. "Why tell me all this?" They asked. "Why add such a stipulation when you're the one who controls that outcome?"

"You're still a person," Hyacinth replied. "You are a criminal, yes. There is a part of me that wants to ensure you never see the sun again, real or fake," They admitted. "But I hope this talk, as well as some time to think, will make you rethink your methods, as it is making me rethink my own."

The unicorn looked at them skeptically, but said nothing.

"Now, I have one last question for you; an important one," Hyacinth said. "Those weapons. Where did you get them?"


The unicorn blinked. "The..." They blinked. "Well... We bought them from a pegasus. I don't really know much about her," They expressed.

Hyacinth leered. "Do not make me pry. This is a matter of considerable importance," They expressed.

The unicorn threw their hooves up. "I mean it!" They expressed. "All I know is that she was fast! We ordered three axes and she was gone and back before the light shut off for the night!"

Hyacinth sighed. "Do you remember anything else about her?" They asked.

"She had kind of a raspy voice," The unicorn replied. "Kept herself really secret otherwise, though. Form fitting clothing, no mane or tail poking out, even kept her eyes behind mirrored goggles."

"That's not much to go on," They admitted. "But I appreciate you telling me what you know. I must be going soon to tend to my other duties," They expressed, standing up with a grunt.


The unicorn blinked. "How... Long am I going to be here?" They asked.

"I haven't yet decided," Hyacinth conceded. "But for as long as you are here, you will be taken care of like any other under our protection. Once the others are up and well, I will see about having you moved to a shared cell so you're not alone," They commented. "If you remember anything else about your supplier, let one of the puppets know," They expressed, pointing to a single Herald sitting alongside the regular pony guards in the hallway.

"Yeah... Of course, your highness," The unicorn replied, waving slackly at them as they left.
