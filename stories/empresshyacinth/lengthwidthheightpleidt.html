Into The Breach
Abomination (DnD)

Hyacinth heaved gently as they retired to their room for the night, smiling as they saw Cardion and Ester with Allium and Solace. The two fillies had already grown considerably in the span of less than a month; still smaller than Cardion, but now nearing half her height. "Hello my dears," They greeted tenderly, scooping up Allium and Solace into a hug. "Oh, goodness... You two are getting heavy," They chuckled, nuzzling the two.

"Haba," Solace babbled, reaching up to bop Hyacinth's chin with her front hooves.

"Yes, heavy," Hyacinth replied, giggling as the little hoof connected with their muzzle.


"Was work kind to mother today?" Ester asked.

"Oh, yes, things are alright right now, dear. No major crises, nothing problematic coming up," Hyacinth expressed. "That's why I'm back a bit earlier than usual."

Cardion stood up, joining in the hug. "This one is glad things are settling a bit," She expressed.

"So am I, dear," Hyacinth replied, nuzzling Cardion. "A quiet night like tonight is good to give us some time to think."

"What is mother going to think about?" Ester asked.

"Ah, well... I wanted to try meditating," Hyacinth conceded, gently setting the foals down as they rolled forward, laying down on their belly. "I have been thinking about the twins' birthday... For a few moments, I could see a giant tree, extending from me and outwards in every direction. I saw how it connected to Cardion and the twins, and the other children in the room. I wish to try to go back to that place," They expressed.

"Does mother need help?" Ester inquired.

"Just peace and quiet, my dears. It takes focus to control my sight in that way," Hyacinth expressed, closing their eyes.


The room went very quiet as the Archons attempted to accommodate Hyacinth's request. Even Solace, rambunctious as she was, sat neatly before her mother, caught in the lower-energy brainwaves as Hyacinth attempted to steady theirself.

It was shaky at first, slow. Only dimly did the tree appear, and they couldn't see much further than the trunk around them. They continued to focus, however.

They fell further into whatever trance they were pressing into. Slowly, the tree grew upwards and outwards, becoming clearer and more defined as their eyes focused in the new direction. Finally, they saw it again. The equid figure sliding along the branches, wrapped around them like a blanket. Hyacinth allowed their vision to settle on the creature, just watching for a time. They watched it gently preen away miniscule bits of material, smoothing out the surface as it subtly swayed and flexed.


Finally, the creature seemed to notice it was being watched. It looked up and around. Hyacinth tried to think of a way to signal it. They wondered if it, too, was part of them. They tried reaching out to it gently, attempting to assume control. Something, however, felt wrong. Like they were trying to flex a muscle that wasn't there, like their body was missing pieces. They heaved softly, a bit dejected at that feeling. Their eyes tensed as they tried to figure out what they needed, frustration building. The fact they were not the master of their own body reared its ugly head yet again; machinations beyond their grasp were evidently toiling, but the infuriating fact remained that they could not yet seize them.


Finally, their creature noticed their standing. It slid down towards them, gracefully moving along the tree.

"There you are..." Hyacinth murmured softly. "I was worried I'd never see you again."

The creature slid around the trunk, always within reach and view, but never more tangible than a current in the air.

Hyacinth considered this. They wondered if the creature was in the same predicament as them: Unable to reach out to touch the other. "Do you need help touching this world?" They asked.

The creature looked at them for a moment. It slid down, reaching out one of its luminous pink hooves to touch Hyacinth. Hyacinth attempted to line their hoof up with it. Hyacinth felt theirself trying to move their hoof in unusual directions; toward the creature was not a direction they could describe in space. Up, down, left, right, forward, backward... Nothing worked. They felt they lacked the muscles to adequately move in such a direction. The creature, however, seemed more inclined to it. The pressure on their hoof grew greater and greater.

Finally, in one sharp movement, the creature lurched away from the tree. It fell into Hyacinth, churning inside their body in strange ways.


Hyacinth opened their eyes. A pupa had landed next to them; different only in that its contents glowed faintly as the pupa swelled dramatically. It seemed to be a large puppet; smaller than an Archon, but not by much. Perhaps similar in height to a Praetor.

The puppet that hatched was nothing short of a strange creature: Its body seemed separated into two "halves" of an over and undercoat, split by a seam that started from the upper lip and ran all the way down to past the puppet's tail. Between the layers of body was the luminous figure of the creature. Much of it was obfuscated by the physical body, but the flaps of its blanket-like torso hung down, its original legs dangling uselessly at its sides. Hyacinth's eyes widened as the creature open it's: Sclerae and pupils looked at them, but no irises.


Ester and Cardion both stood up urgently, each taking one of the twins to protect them. Hyacinth, however, simply stared at the creature. Pieces began to fall into place as memories were shaken free of dust.

"...Means no harm..." The creature said. Its voice was high, wispy, and very faint.

Hyacinth slowly stood up, taking a deep breath. "There you are," They greeted, pulling the strange creature into a hug.

The creature hesitated at first, but hugged back, seemingly having trouble adjusting to its new body. Its hooves jerked in strange ways, awkwardly smacking against Hyacinth rather than smoothly. Hyacinth chuckled. "Not so easy, is it?" They asked gently.

The creature briefly looked up at them, puffing softly. "...Heavy," It murmured.

Hyacinth grinned. "We are quite heavy here," They agreed.


Cardion had relaxed by now, just holding Allium laxily in her front legs. Ester still seemed wary, holding Solace close.

"It's okay, dear," Hyacinth promised, looking at Ester. "I know she's not familiar, but she's family just the same," They expressed.

"Who is this, mother?" Cardion asked, looking at the strange, pupilled creature.

Hyacinth felt their heart quiver as they tried to manifest the answer to that question. It was a simple question, and yet the answer hit them like a ton of bricks. "This..." They began taking a shaky breath. "...Is Archie."
